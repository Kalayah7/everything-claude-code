<p><b>For the mind that will not slow down, and the heart that feels guilty about it.</b></p>
<p>You lie awake going over what might happen. Your chest tightens for reasons you cannot always name. And on top of the anxiety, you carry a second weight: the sense that a real Christian would not feel this way.</p>
<p><i>A Crowd of Thoughts</i> is a short, gentle book that begins somewhere different: <b>anxiety is not proof that your faith has failed.</b> The Bible is full of people who were afraid and overwhelmed, and God met every one of them:</p>

<li><b>David</b>, who prayed "when I am afraid", not "if"</li>
<li><b>Elijah</b>, exhausted under a tree, who was given sleep and food before he was given a single word of correction</li>
<li><b>Paul</b>, who wrote "in nothing be anxious" from a prison cell, and admitted his own daily anxiety</li>
<li><b>Jesus in Gethsemane</b>, sorrowful to the point of death, asking his friends to stay awake with him</li>
<li><b>The disciples in the storm</b>, asking "don't you care?"</li>
</ul>
<p>Twelve short chapters, each with two honest questions and a brief prayer. Simple practices for racing minds: naming the crowd, the casting list, looking at the birds, praying with your breath, checking the stories your mind tells. A whole chapter explains why seeing a doctor or counselor is wise, not faithless. A six-week discussion guide makes it easy to read with a friend or small group.</p>
<p>Book 2 in the Middle Seasons series, after <i>In the Waiting</i>.</p>
<p><i>This book is for faith and reflection. It is not medical care or counseling and does not diagnose or treat anxiety. Scripture quotations are from the World English Bible (public domain). Written with the help of AI tools and reviewed and edited by people.</i></p>

# 3. Under the Tree

Elijah had just had the greatest day of his life.

On Mount Carmel, in front of King Ahab and a great crowd, he had challenged the prophets of Baal. Fire had fallen from heaven. The people had fallen on their faces and cried out that the LORD is God. After three years of drought, the rain had finally come.

And then Queen Jezebel sent him a message. By this time tomorrow, she said, you will be dead.

Elijah ran.

## The crash

He ran south, out of the kingdom, all the way to Beersheba. He left his servant there and went on alone. Then he prayed this:

{{verse: 1 Kings 19:4}}

It is one of the most painful prayers in the Bible. The prophet who had just called down fire from heaven now asked God to let him die.

If you have ever felt something like this, please pause here. You are not the first, and you are not beyond help. If you are having thoughts of ending your life, please tell someone today: a doctor, a counselor, a pastor, a friend. In the United States, you can call or text 988 at any time, day or night. Elsewhere, call your local emergency number or a crisis line.

## What God did first

What strikes many readers is what God did *not* do next.

He did not rebuke Elijah. He did not remind him of the fire on Carmel, or tell him that a man of his faith should know better. He did not give him a sermon.

Instead, Elijah lay down and slept under the tree. And then:

{{verse: 1 Kings 19:5-6}}

Then the angel came a second time:

{{verse: 1 Kings 19:7}}

Sleep. Food. Water. More sleep. More food. And a simple acknowledgment: *the journey is too great for you*.

## The body matters

There is great gentleness in this. Before God said anything to Elijah about his calling, his fears or his future, he cared for his body.

Elijah was exhausted. He had been through enormous stress, run a long way, and gone alone into the desert. The text sets his despair right beside his exhaustion, and God treated both.

Anxious people sometimes forget that they have bodies. We try to solve everything in our heads, praying harder, thinking harder, while running on little sleep, skipped meals and endless adrenaline. And then we wonder why our minds will not settle.

This story gives you permission to start with the simple things. Sleep, if you can. Eat something. Drink water. Step outside. Ask for help with the practical load. These are not unspiritual. In Elijah's story, they were the first thing God offered. If your despair runs as deep as Elijah's, the first simple thing is to tell a doctor, a counselor or a crisis line today.

## Later, a quiet voice

Strengthened by that food, Elijah traveled forty days to Horeb, the mountain of God. There, God met him again. There was a great wind, then an earthquake, then a fire, but the text says God was not in any of them. After the fire came something else: a still, small voice, or as some translate it, a sound of sheer silence.

God asked him, before and after the quiet, what he was doing there, and let him pour out his complaint both times. He told him that he was not alone, as he had believed. And he gave him a companion, Elisha, to walk with him.

Rest came first. Then the quiet voice. Then company. That is not a formula, but it is a gentle order, and it may be one worth following.

## For the quiet minute

1. How is your body doing right now? When did you last sleep well, eat properly or get outside?
2. Is "the journey is too great for you" true for you at the moment? Who could help carry some of it?

*God, I am tired, and the journey is too great for me. Care for me the way you cared for Elijah. Help me rest and eat and accept help. And when I am ready, let me hear your quiet voice. Amen.*

# 12. My Peace

It was the night before he died.

Jesus was with his disciples in an upstairs room in Jerusalem. He had washed their feet. He had told them that one of them would betray him, and that Peter would deny him. He had told them he was going away. The room must have been thick with confusion and fear.

And into that room, John's Gospel tells us, Jesus spoke these words:

{{verse: John 14:27}}

## Peace on the worst night

The timing of these words matters. Jesus did not say this on a peaceful afternoon by the lake. He said it on the night his disciples' world was about to fall apart, knowing that in a few hours he would be arrested, and that most of them would run.

He did not tell them that nothing bad would happen. A little later the same evening, he said plainly that in the world they would have trouble. He knew what was coming, for him and for them.

But he gave them his peace. *My peace I give to you.* Not a peace that depended on everything going well, but his own peace, the peace he carried into Gethsemane and to the cross.

## Not as the world gives

Jesus said his peace was different from the peace the world gives.

The world's peace usually depends on circumstances. We feel peaceful when the bills are paid, the test results are clear, the relationship is mended, the danger is past. That kind of peace is good, and we can be thankful for it. But it is fragile. It can vanish with a phone call.

The peace Jesus offers is rooted somewhere else: in his presence and his love, which do not change with circumstances. It can exist alongside trouble. It is the peace of the one asleep in the storm, of the one who prayed *not what I desire, but what you desire* in the garden.

This does not mean you will always *feel* peaceful. Feelings come and go, especially for anxious people. But it means there is a peace available that does not depend on your feelings or your circumstances, because it depends on him.

## Perfect peace

The prophet Isaiah wrote, many centuries earlier:

{{verse: Isaiah 26:3}}

*Whose mind is steadfast.* Older translations say "whose mind is stayed on thee". It is a picture of a mind that keeps returning to God, the way a boat on a mooring keeps swinging back to its anchor point.

This is not a promise that only people with perfectly focused minds get peace. Anxious minds wander constantly. It is a picture of where peace is found: in returning, again and again, to God. Every time you notice your mind racing and turn it back to him, even for a moment, you are staying your mind on him.

## The crowd and the peace

This book began with a crowd of thoughts and the comfort that came in the middle of them. It ends with the peace of Jesus, given on the most frightening night of his friends' lives.

Your mind may still be crowded. It may stay crowded for a long time. Anxiety is often not something we defeat once and for all, but something we learn to live with, manage and bring to God, again and again.

But you are not alone in the crowd. The one who said *don't let your heart be troubled* also said *my peace I give to you*. He did not say it to people who had their fear under control. He said it to frightened friends on a dark night. He says it to you.

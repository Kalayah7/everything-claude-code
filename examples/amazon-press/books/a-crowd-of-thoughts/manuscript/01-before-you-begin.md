# Before You Begin

This book is for the mind that will not slow down.

Perhaps you lie awake going over conversations that already happened, or rehearsing ones that may never happen. Perhaps your chest tightens in the parking lot at work, or at the sound of your phone. Perhaps you can name exactly what you are worried about. Or perhaps it is a low hum of dread that has been running so long you have forgotten what quiet feels like.

And perhaps, on top of the anxiety itself, you carry a second weight: the sense that a real Christian would not feel this way. You have heard the verses. *Do not be anxious about anything.* You have tried. And still your mind races, and now you feel anxious *and* guilty about being anxious.

If that is you, this book is for you, and the first thing it wants to say is this: anxiety is not proof that your faith has failed.

The Bible is full of people who were afraid, overwhelmed, sleepless and sick with worry. Some of them are its heroes. One of them asked to die. One of them, on the night before his death, was so distressed that he asked his closest friends to stay awake with him. That one was Jesus. These people are not in Scripture as warnings. They are there, in part, so that frightened people would know that God meets them.

## What this book is, and is not

This is a short book. Each chapter looks at one passage of Scripture, in its real setting, and asks what it has to say to an anxious mind. At the end of each chapter, under the heading *For the quiet minute*, you will find two questions and a short prayer. Use them, adapt them or skip them. There is a guide at the back for reading the book with a friend or a group.

This book is not a treatment for anxiety. Anxiety can be a season, a reaction to something genuinely frightening, or a medical condition, and often it is some of each. If your anxiety is stopping you from sleeping, eating, working or enjoying anything, or if it comes with panic, please talk to a doctor or a licensed counselor. Chapter 10 is entirely about why doing that is wise, and not a lack of faith.

If you ever have thoughts of ending your life, please reach out right away. In the United States you can call or text 988 at any time. Elsewhere, please contact your local emergency number or a crisis line, or tell someone near you.

## How to read it

Slowly. One chapter at a time, if that helps. Bible quotations come from the World English Bible, a modern public-domain translation, which uses the name Yahweh where many translations print "the LORD".

You do not need to be calm to read this book. You do not need to finish it in order. You can read it in the middle of the noise, which is, after all, where most anxious people do their praying.

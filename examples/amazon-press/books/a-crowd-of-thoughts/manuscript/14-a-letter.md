# A Letter for the Racing Mind

Dear friend,

If your mind is still crowded as you reach the end of this book, that is all right. This book was never going to quiet it for good. Anxiety rarely leaves because we read the right chapter.

But perhaps a few things have shifted. Here is what this book hopes you will keep:

Your anxiety is not proof that your faith has failed. David was afraid. Elijah asked to die. Paul carried anxiety every day. Jesus was overwhelmed with sorrow in the garden. God met every one of them.

You can pray from the middle of the noise. Bring the whole crowd.

When you are afraid, you can still turn toward God. Fear and trust can share a sentence.

Your body matters. Sleep, food, water, a walk, a slow breath: God cared for Elijah that way before he said anything else.

Philippians 4:6 is an invitation, written in chains. Bring everything. God's peace stands guard.

Throw your worries onto God, because he cares for you. Then throw them again.

Look at the birds. Come back to today.

Getting help is not faithlessness. It is wisdom, and often it is how God answers.

Come back to what is true, as many times as it takes.

And when your heart is troubled, remember that Jesus gave his peace to frightened friends on the darkest night of their lives. He has not taken it back.

{{verse: Psalm 94:18-19}}

May his comforts delight your soul, in the middle of the crowd.

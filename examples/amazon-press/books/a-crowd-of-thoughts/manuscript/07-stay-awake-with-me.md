# 6. Stay Awake With Me

On the last night before his death, after supper with his disciples, Jesus went out to a garden called Gethsemane, on the Mount of Olives.

He left most of the disciples near the entrance and took three of them, Peter, James and John, a little further. Then Matthew tells us this:

{{verse: Matthew 26:37-38}}

Sorrowful and severely troubled. Exceedingly sorrowful, even to death. These are some of the strongest words in the Gospels, and they are used of Jesus.

## Jesus in distress

Christians believe Jesus is the Son of God, the one who calmed storms and raised the dead. And here he is, in a garden at night, so distressed that he says his sorrow is enough to kill him.

Mark's Gospel says he began to be greatly troubled and distressed. Luke's Gospel adds that, in his anguish, his sweat became like great drops of blood falling to the ground.

Whatever else this scene means, it means this: overwhelming distress is not foreign to God. Jesus knew what it is to dread what is coming, to feel the weight of it in his body, to be unable to rest.

If you have ever felt your heart pounding in the dark, unable to stop your mind from running ahead to what tomorrow will bring, you are not alone in that garden.

## What he did with it

Look at what Jesus did with his distress.

**He said it out loud.** He did not hide it from his friends. He told them plainly that his soul was overwhelmed.

**He asked for company.** *Stay here, and watch with me.* The Son of God asked his friends to stay awake with him. He did not want to be alone.

**He prayed honestly.** He fell on his face and prayed that, if it were possible, the cup might pass from him. He asked for what he wanted: a way out.

**He surrendered.** And then he added, *nevertheless, not what I desire, but what you desire.* He kept his request and his trust together.

**He kept praying.** Matthew says he went back and prayed the same prayer three times.

None of these steps made his distress disappear. He still went to the cross. But he did not face the night alone, and he did not stop talking to his Father.

## When your friends fall asleep

There is a painful detail in this story. Three times Jesus came back to his friends, and three times he found them asleep.

{{verse: Matthew 26:40}}

He had asked for company, and his friends could not stay awake. They were exhausted, overwhelmed with sorrow of their own, Luke suggests. But the result was the same: in his worst hour, Jesus was largely alone.

If you have reached out to people in your anxiety and found them distracted, dismissive or simply asleep, Jesus knows that disappointment too. It is real, and it hurts.

It is still worth asking. Jesus did not stop asking his friends to watch with him. And some of the people around you may be able to stay awake better than you expect, if they know how much you need it.

## For the quiet minute

1. What is the "cup" you would like God to take away? Ask for it honestly.
2. Who could you ask to "stay awake with you" in this season? What would you ask them for?

*Jesus, you knew distress in the garden. You know mine. I am asking you to take this cup from me: [name it]. And if you do not, stay with me in it. Help me not to be alone tonight. Amen.*

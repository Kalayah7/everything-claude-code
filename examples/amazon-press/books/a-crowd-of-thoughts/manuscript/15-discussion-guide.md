# Discussion Guide

This guide is for reading *A Crowd of Thoughts* with a friend, a small group or a class over six weeks, two chapters a week. Each session takes about an hour.

A note for leaders: anxiety is common, and some people in your group may be struggling more than they show. Let people share as much or as little as they want. Never suggest that anxiety means weak faith, and resist quick fixes. If someone mentions panic attacks, being unable to function, or any thoughts of self-harm, follow up privately, encourage them to see a doctor or counselor, and make sure they know about crisis help: in the United States, call or text 988; elsewhere, a local crisis line.

## Week 1: A Crowd of Thoughts and When, Not If (Before You Begin, chapters 1 and 2)

- **Open:** What does a "crowded mind" feel like for you?
- **Read together:** Psalm 94:16-19 and Psalm 56:3-4, 8.
- **Discuss:**
  1. Have you ever felt you needed to calm down before you could pray? How does Psalm 94:19 speak to that?
  2. What do you make of David saying *when* I am afraid rather than *if*?
  3. How can fear and trust exist at the same time?
- **Practice:** Each day this week, write down the crowd of thoughts and bring the whole list to God.

## Week 2: Elijah and Paul (chapters 3 and 4)

- **Open:** When you are stressed, what happens to your sleep and eating?
- **Read together:** 1 Kings 19:3-8 and Philippians 4:4-7.
- **Discuss:**
  1. Why do you think God cared for Elijah's body before speaking to him about anything else?
  2. Has Philippians 4:6 ever made you feel worse? How does knowing Paul's situation change how you read it?
  3. What does it mean for God's peace to "guard" your heart?
- **Practice:** Choose one way to care for your body this week, and one list of requests and thanks.

## Week 3: Casting and the Garden (chapters 5 and 6)

- **Open:** Is there a worry you keep picking back up?
- **Read together:** 1 Peter 5:6-7 and Matthew 26:36-46.
- **Discuss:**
  1. What makes it hard to believe that God cares for you?
  2. What strikes you about Jesus's distress in Gethsemane?
  3. Jesus asked his friends to stay awake with him. Who do you ask for help, and how?
- **Practice:** Try the "casting list" from chapter 5.

## Week 4: The Storm and the Lilies (chapters 7 and 8)

- **Open:** Describe a time when God seemed to be asleep.
- **Read together:** Mark 4:35-41 and Matthew 6:25-34.
- **Discuss:**
  1. How do you hear the disciples' question, *don't you care?* Have you asked it?
  2. Why do you think Jesus tells us to *look* at the birds and *consider* the lilies?
  3. What does "worry cannot add one moment" free you from?
- **Practice:** Spend one minute a day this week looking at something living.

## Week 5: The Body and Getting Help (chapters 9 and 10)

- **Open:** Where in your body do you feel stress?
- **Read together:** Psalm 139:13-14, Psalm 4:8 and Proverbs 15:22.
- **Discuss:**
  1. How does seeing your body as "fearfully and wonderfully made" change how you think about anxiety?
  2. What messages have you heard in church about counseling or medication for anxiety?
  3. How can a group like this support someone who is getting help?
- **Practice:** Try praying with your breathing once a day.

## Week 6: Whatever Is True and My Peace (chapters 11 and 12, and the letter)

- **Open:** What frightening story has your mind told you recently?
- **Read together:** Philippians 4:8-9, John 14:25-27 and Isaiah 26:3.
- **Discuss:**
  1. How is "think about whatever is true" different from positive thinking?
  2. What is the difference between the world's peace and the peace Jesus gives?
  3. What has helped you most from this book?
- **Close:** Pray for one another by name, for peace in the middle of whatever is crowding each person's mind.

# 7. Asleep in the Storm

One evening, after a long day of teaching by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus said to his disciples, *Let's go over to the other side.* They set out in a boat.

A great windstorm arose. The waves broke into the boat, and it began to fill with water.

Several of these men were fishermen. They had spent their lives on this lake. They knew what weather could do, and they knew when to be afraid. This was one of those times.

And Jesus was asleep.

## "Don't you care?"

Mark's Gospel tells us exactly where he was: in the stern, asleep on a cushion. The disciples woke him, and their words are not polite:

{{verse: Mark 4:38}}

*Don't you care?*

That question is at the heart of a great deal of anxiety. Not only *what will happen?* but *does anyone care? Does God see this? Why is he silent while I am going under?*

The disciples asked it out loud, to his face. And the Gospels recorded it, without cleaning it up.

## The calm

Jesus woke up. He rebuked the wind and said to the sea, *Peace! Be still!* The wind stopped, and there was a great calm.

Then he turned to his disciples:

{{verse: Mark 4:40}}

It is tempting to hear this as a scolding, and perhaps there is a rebuke in it. But notice when it comes: *after* he has calmed the storm. He does not leave them in the waves until they find enough faith. He deals with the storm first, and then asks his question.

And it is a question, not a verdict. *Why are you so afraid?* It invites them to think about what they had just seen, and about who was in the boat with them.

The disciples' response is telling. Mark says they were terrified, and asked each other, *Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?* Their fear changed shape. It became awe.

## When Jesus seems asleep

Many anxious Christians know the feeling of a Jesus who seems to be asleep. You have prayed. The waves are still coming over the side. Heaven feels quiet.

This story does not promise that every storm will stop the moment you pray. Jesus calmed this storm, but the disciples would face many more, and some of them would not end in calm.

But it does say a few things worth holding on to.

**He was in the boat.** Even asleep, Jesus was not somewhere else. He had said, *Let's go over to the other side*, and he was going with them.

**You can wake him.** The disciples' panicked, accusing prayer was heard. You can pray *don't you care?* too.

**His sleep was not indifference.** He slept because he was at rest, not because he did not care. His peace in the storm was not a sign that he had abandoned them. It was a sign of who he was.

## Sharing his rest

Some people find it helpful, in an anxious moment, to picture this scene: the storm, the water, and Jesus, at rest in the back of the boat. Not to shame themselves for not being calm, but to remember that there is a peace bigger than their own, and that it is in the boat with them.

You may not be able to sleep through your storm. Most of us cannot. But you can ask the one who can to be near you in it.

## For the quiet minute

1. Where in your life does it feel like Jesus is asleep? Ask him your honest question.
2. What does it mean to you that Jesus was in the boat, even while he slept?

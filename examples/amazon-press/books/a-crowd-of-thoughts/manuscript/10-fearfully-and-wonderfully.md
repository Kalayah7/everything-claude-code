# 9. Fearfully and Wonderfully Made

Anxiety is not only something that happens in the mind. It happens in the body.

A racing heart. Shallow breathing. Tight shoulders. A knot in the stomach. Sweaty palms. Trouble sleeping. Many people with anxiety notice these things first, before they can even name what they are worried about. For some, the physical symptoms are so strong that they fear something is seriously wrong with their health, which makes the anxiety worse.

If that is you, two things are worth saying. First, if you have physical symptoms that worry you, please see a doctor and get them checked. That is wise, not faithless. Second, whatever the cause, your body is not your enemy. It is part of how God made you.

## Knit together

Psalm 139 is a prayer of wonder at how closely God knows and holds a person. At its center are these lines:

{{verse: Psalm 139:13-14}}

The psalmist does not see the body as something separate from faith or less important than the soul. The body was knit together by God, carefully, in secret. It is *fearfully and wonderfully made*.

That includes the parts of us that react to danger. The quick heartbeat and fast breathing that come with fear are, in themselves, part of a remarkable design that helps us respond when something threatens us. In anxiety, that system can become overactive, sounding the alarm when there is no fire. That can be miserable and exhausting. But it is not evidence that you are broken beyond repair, or that God has made a mistake.

## Lying down in peace

Psalm 4 ends with a prayer many people have said at bedtime:

{{verse: Psalm 4:8}}

Sleep is one of the first things anxiety steals, and one of the things anxious people most need. This verse does not promise that you will fall asleep instantly. But it ties rest to safety in God. *You alone make me live in safety.* Sleep becomes an act of trust: letting go of watchfulness for a few hours, because someone else is watching.

## Caring for the body you were given

The Bible does not give a list of health instructions for anxiety, and this book will not either. A doctor can advise you on what is right for your body. But Scripture does show God caring for bodies, as we saw with Elijah under the juniper tree, and many people with anxiety find that simple, steady care for the body helps the mind too. Things like:

- **Sleep**, with a regular bedtime and a little time without screens before it.
- **Food and water**, at regular times, even when you do not feel hungry.
- **Moving**, such as a walk outside, even a short one.
- **Slow breathing**, especially letting the breath out slowly, when you notice your breathing has become fast and shallow.
- **Less of what makes it worse**, which for some people includes a lot of caffeine or alcohol, or endless news and scrolling.

They are not a treatment for anxiety, and they do not replace one. They are not spiritual achievements either, and you are not failing if you cannot manage them all. They are ways of honoring the body God knit together, and giving it a fair chance to settle.

## Praying with the body

Some people find it helps to pray with their breathing. For example, breathe in slowly while saying silently, *You alone*, and breathe out slowly while saying, *make me live in safety.* Or use any short phrase from Scripture that steadies you. Do it for a minute or two.

It is a very old way of praying. It brings the body and the mind together before God, instead of leaving one of them behind.

## For the quiet minute

1. Where do you feel anxiety in your body? How has it affected your sleep, eating or energy?
2. What is one small way you could care for your body this week as an act of trust?

*God, you knit me together. My body is anxious, and it is tired. Help me care for it the way you do. Let me lie down tonight in peace, because you alone make me live in safety. Amen.*

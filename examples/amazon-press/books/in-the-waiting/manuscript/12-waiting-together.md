# 11. Waiting Together

Early in their time in the wilderness, the Israelites were attacked by the Amalekites. Joshua led the people out to fight, while Moses climbed a hill with the staff of God in his hand.

The book of Exodus tells us that while Moses held up his hands, Israel prevailed. When he lowered them, the Amalekites began to win. And Moses' arms got tired. Of course they did. He was a man, not a statue.

So two other men, Aaron and Hur, did something very simple:

{{verse: Exodus 17:12}}

They found him a rock to sit on. They stood on either side of him, and each of them held up one of his arms. They stayed there until the sun went down.

## Not meant to do it alone

I love this story because it is so practical. Moses had the staff, the calling, the central role. But he could not keep his arms up all day. Nobody could. He needed a rock to sit on and two friends to hold his arms.

Waiting is tiring in a way that is hard to explain to people who are not in it. It wears you down slowly. You may be able to hold your arms up for a while, keep praying, keep hoping, keep going to work or church or the doctor's office. But at some point, your arms get heavy.

You were not meant to do this alone. The Bible's picture of faith is not of lonely heroes, but of a people, a family, a body, in which members carry each other.

{{verse: Galatians 6:2}}

## Why it is hard to let people in

If waiting together is so important, why do so many of us wait alone?

Sometimes it is because we are tired of explaining. Every update feels like another disappointment to report. *No, nothing yet. No, still no news.*

Sometimes it is because people have said unhelpful things. Like Hannah's husband, they have tried to fix it, or measure it, or hurry it along. Like Job's friends in the Bible, they have tried to explain it, and their explanations have hurt.

Sometimes it is because we feel we should be over it by now, and we are embarrassed to still be struggling.

All of these are understandable. But they can leave us holding our arms up alone on the hill, getting weaker, while the people who might help do not even know we are struggling.

## Finding your Aaron and Hur

You probably do not need a crowd. Moses had two. Think about who in your life might be able to hold up your arms. Not someone who will fix things, but someone who will stay. Here are a few things you might ask of them:

- **"Can I tell you how I'm really doing, and you just listen?"** Many people want to help but do not know how. Telling them what you need is a gift to them.
- **"Will you pray for me on the hard days?"** You might tell them which days are hard: the anniversary, the appointment, the holiday.
- **"Can you check in with me now and then, even if I go quiet?"** Waiting can make us withdraw. A friend who reaches out anyway can make a great difference.
- **"Will you sit with me?"** Sometimes the most helpful thing is simply company, a rock to sit on and someone beside you.

If you have no one like this right now, consider whether a pastor, a counselor or a support group might be a starting point. There is no shame in that. Moses did not choose who stood beside him on the hill. He just let them hold his arms.

## Being someone's Aaron or Hur

There is another side to this. The people around you are waiting too. Someone in your church is waiting for a diagnosis. Someone at work is waiting for their marriage to heal. Someone in your family is waiting for something they have never told you about.

One of the gifts of a long middle is that it can make us better at standing beside others. We learn what helps and what does not. We learn that "I'm so sorry, I'm here" is better than any explanation.

{{verse: Romans 12:15}}

You may not have the strength to hold up someone else's arms for a whole day. But you may be able to send a message. Sit with someone for an hour. Remember their hard day and ask how it went. In the middle of your own waiting, that can be its own kind of manna, for them and for you.

## For the waiting room

1. Who could be an Aaron or Hur for you in this season? What could you ask them for?
2. Who around you is waiting, and how could you hold up their arms, even a little?

*God, my arms are tired. Give me people who will hold them up, and the courage to let them. Show me who I can stand beside too. Amen.*

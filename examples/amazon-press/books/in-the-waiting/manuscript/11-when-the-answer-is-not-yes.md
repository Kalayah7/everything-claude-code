# 10. When the Answer Is Not Yes

This is the chapter I most wanted to write and least wanted to write.

Many books about waiting tell the stories of people who received what they waited for. Abraham got his son. Hannah got her son. Joseph got out of prison. Lazarus walked out of the tomb. These are true stories, and they are good ones. But if they were the only stories, they would quietly tell you something that is not true: that if you wait long enough and faithfully enough, you will always get what you asked for.

The Bible does not say that. And it would be dishonest of me to let you finish this book thinking it does.

## Three times

The apostle Paul had something he called a thorn in the flesh. We do not know what it was. People have guessed at an illness, a disability, a persistent opponent, an eye condition. Paul does not tell us. He only tells us that it tormented him, and that he prayed about it:

{{verse: 2 Corinthians 12:8-9}}

Paul asked three times. He was an apostle, a man of extraordinary faith who had seen God do remarkable things. If anyone could have prayed something away, it was Paul. And the answer was no.

Not "not yet". No. Instead of removing the thorn, God gave Paul something else: grace that was enough, and power that showed up in weakness.

I want to be honest that this is a hard answer. Paul seems to have come to accept it, and even to find a strange strength in it. But that does not mean it was easy, and it does not mean you must feel the same way the moment you realise the answer might be no. Paul asked three times before he heard it. Many of us will ask far more.

## Died in faith

The eleventh chapter of the letter to the Hebrews is sometimes called the hall of faith. It lists many of the people we have met in this book, and others: Abel, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses. In the middle of that list, the writer pauses to say this:

{{verse: Hebrews 11:13}}

They died not having received the promises.

Toward the end of the chapter, the list changes tone. It speaks of people who saw great victories, and then of others who were mocked, imprisoned, beaten, who wandered about in poverty, who were mistreated. And it ends by saying that all these, though commended for their faith, did not receive what was promised in their lifetimes.

These people are not in the hall of faith *despite* not receiving. They are there partly *because* they kept faith when they did not receive. Their waiting did not end the way they hoped, in this life. And Scripture holds them up as heroes.

## What this does not mean

It does not mean your prayers do not matter. Paul prayed, and his praying was part of his relationship with God, part of how he came to hear what he heard.

It does not mean you should stop hoping or stop asking. Jesus himself taught his followers to keep asking, keep seeking, keep knocking. Hannah asked for years. The psalmists asked how long, over and over.

It does not mean that a no was your fault. Paul's thorn was not a punishment for weak faith. The people in Hebrews 11 did not fail to receive because they did something wrong.

And it does not mean that God has stopped caring. Paul's no came with grace. The faithful in Hebrews 11 were looking forward, the writer says, to a better country, and God was not ashamed to be called their God.

## Living with a possible no

Some of you reading this already know that the answer is no. The person has died. The door has closed for good. The body will not be what it was. That is grief, and it is its own long road. Please be gentle with yourself on it, and please let others walk it with you, including a counselor or pastor if it is weighing heavily.

Others of you do not know yet. The answer might be yes, or later, or no. For you, I think the stories of Paul and of Hebrews 11 offer a kind of freedom. You do not have to hold your faith hostage to one outcome. You can ask boldly for what you want and still know that God will be God, and you will still be held, even if the answer is not yes.

That is not a small thing. It might be the most important thing this book has to say.

## For the waiting room

1. What would it mean for you if the answer turned out to be no? What do you fear about that?
2. Is there a way your faith has grown in this season that does not depend on getting what you asked for?

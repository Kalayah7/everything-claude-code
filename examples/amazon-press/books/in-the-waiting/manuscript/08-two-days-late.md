# 7. Two Days Late

There is a verse in the Gospel of John that does not seem to fit with the verse that follows it.

Jesus had close friends in the village of Bethany: two sisters, Martha and Mary, and their brother Lazarus. When Lazarus became seriously ill, the sisters sent word to Jesus. Their message was simple. The one you love is sick.

Here is how John describes what happened next:

{{verse: John 11:5-6}}

Read those two sentences again. Jesus loved them. *Therefore,* or *so,* he stayed where he was two more days.

## Love and delay in the same breath

If I had written that passage, I would have put a different word in the middle. Jesus loved them, *but* he stayed. Or, Jesus loved them, *and yet* he stayed. That is how delay usually feels from the inside: like a contradiction of love. If God loved me, surely he would come. Surely he would hurry.

John does not write it that way. He puts love and delay side by side, and he does not apologise. The delay is not presented as evidence that Jesus did not care. It is presented as something that happened while he cared.

By the time Jesus arrived in Bethany, Lazarus had been dead and in the tomb for four days.

## If you had been here

Martha heard that Jesus was coming and went out to meet him on the road. Her first words to him were these:

{{verse: John 11:21}}

Later, Mary came out to him, fell at his feet, and said exactly the same thing.

I imagine those sisters had said that sentence to each other many times over those four days. *If he had been here.* *Why didn't he come?* It is the prayer of everyone who has waited for God and felt that he arrived too late, or not at all.

Jesus did not rebuke them for saying it. He did not tell them their grief showed a lack of faith. With Martha, he talked. With Mary, something else happened.

When Jesus saw Mary weeping, and the people with her weeping, he was deeply moved and troubled. He asked where they had laid Lazarus. They said, come and see.

And then comes the shortest verse in most English Bibles:

{{verse: John 11:35}}

## Why did he weep?

This has always puzzled readers, and it should. According to John, Jesus already knew what he was about to do. A few minutes later, he would stand at the tomb and call Lazarus out, alive. So why weep?

I think it tells us something about God that we badly need to know in the middle. Jesus did not stand outside the sisters' grief, impatient for it to be over because he knew the happy ending. He entered into it. He wept with them, at the graveside, before he did anything about it.

Whatever God is doing in your waiting, this story suggests that he is not indifferent to how it feels. The delay is real. The loss is real. And God is not watching from a distance with a stopwatch. In Jesus, he stands beside the grave and weeps.

## An honest word

I need to be careful here. Lazarus was raised. Not everyone's loved one is. Not every waiting ends with the stone rolled away. If you are waiting for someone who has already died, or for a healing that may not come, the end of this story may be painful to read.

Lazarus was raised to this life, which means that one day he would die again. His raising was a sign, a glimpse of something larger that Jesus said about himself: that he is the resurrection and the life. Christians hold that hope for all who die, not because everyone is brought back in this life, but because death does not have the last word.

That is a hope for the end of the story. It does not make the middle painless. Jesus himself did not treat it as painless. He wept.

So, if you are angry or heartbroken that God seems to be two days late, or more, you can say so. Martha and Mary did. And then you can look for him, as they did, coming down the road toward you, and weeping with you when he arrives.

## For the waiting room

1. Is there an "if you had been here" in your story? Say it to God as plainly as Martha did.
2. What difference does it make to you that Jesus wept, even knowing what he would do next?

*Jesus, you loved your friends and still you waited. I don't understand your timing. If you had been here, things might be different. Come to me in the middle of this, and stay with me in it. Amen.*

# 6. Bread for Today

The people of Israel had been out of Egypt for about a month when they started to complain about the food.

They were in the wilderness, between slavery and the land they had been promised. It was the very definition of a middle: they had left one life and not yet arrived at the next, and there was no grocery store in the desert. They told Moses they wished they had died in Egypt, where at least they had sat by pots of meat and eaten bread until they were full.

God's answer was not a rebuke. It was breakfast.

{{verse: Exodus 16:4}}

## Enough for one day

Every morning, the people found a fine, flaky substance on the ground, like frost. They did not know what it was. They called it manna, which sounds like the Hebrew for "What is it?" It tasted, the book of Exodus says, like wafers made with honey.

The instructions were simple. Each person was to gather as much as they needed for that day. Nothing was to be kept until morning.

Some people did not listen. They saved some for the next day, just in case. By morning, it had bred worms and begun to stink.

The only exception was the day before the Sabbath, when they gathered double, and the extra kept for the day of rest. For forty years in the wilderness, this was how they ate: one day at a time.

## Why daily?

It would have been easier, it seems to me, if God had given them a year's supply at once. They would have worried less. They could have planned ahead.

But I think that was partly the point. Manna could not be stockpiled. It could only be trusted. Each morning, the people had to get up and look again for what God would provide that day. Their security was not in a full storehouse. It was in a faithful God who showed up every morning.

Waiting tends to push our minds into the future. *What if this goes on another year? What if the money runs out? What if the scan is bad? What if I'm alone forever?* We try to gather enough strength, enough answers, enough reassurance to get us through every possible tomorrow. And it spoils. We cannot store up tomorrow's grace today.

## Jesus and today's bread

Centuries later, Jesus taught his followers to pray in a way that echoes the manna:

{{verse: Matthew 6:11}}

Not bread for the week. Not bread for the year. Bread for today.

A little later in the same sermon, he said this:

{{verse: Matthew 6:34}}

I find great relief in the honesty of that last line. Jesus does not say that tomorrow will be easy, or that each day will be free of trouble. He says each day has enough of its own. He is not promising us the absence of trouble. He is inviting us to carry one day's worth at a time.

## Living in today

This is not the same as pretending the future does not matter. The Israelites still walked toward the promised land. Joseph still stored grain for the famine years. It is wise to plan, to save, to apply, to book the appointment, to take sensible steps.

But there is a difference between *planning for* tomorrow and *living in* tomorrow. Planning takes an hour and then puts the pen down. Worry goes on all night, gathering manna that will spoil by morning.

Here are a few small ways to practise living in today during a long wait:

- **Ask for today's bread.** When you pray, ask specifically for what you need today. Strength for this conversation. Patience for this afternoon. Sleep tonight.
- **Do today's tasks.** Make a short list of what today requires. Do those things. Let tomorrow's list wait for tomorrow.
- **Notice today's manna.** At the end of the day, name one thing that sustained you. A kind word. A meal. An hour without pain. A moment of quiet. You may find there was more provision than you saw at the time.
- **Put the future down for a while.** If your mind keeps running ahead, try writing your worries down and literally closing the notebook. You can pick them up again tomorrow, if you need to.

The wilderness was not where the Israelites wanted to be. But it was where they learned that God would feed them, one morning at a time, for as long as it took.

## For the waiting room

1. What does "today's bread" look like for you? What do you actually need to get through today?
2. What is one worry about the future you could put down, at least until tomorrow?

*God, give me bread for today. Not all the answers, not a guarantee for next year, but enough for this day. Help me notice what you have already given. Amen.*

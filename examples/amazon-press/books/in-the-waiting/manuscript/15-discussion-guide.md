# Discussion Guide

This guide is for reading *In the Waiting* with a friend, a small group or a class over six weeks, two chapters a week. Each session takes about an hour.

A note for leaders: some people in your group may be waiting for very painful things, such as a child, a healing, a reconciliation or a way out of a hard situation. Let people share as much or as little as they want. Resist the urge to fix, explain or offer quick verses. Your most important job is to make it safe to be honest. If someone seems to be in crisis, follow up privately and help them connect with a pastor, counselor or doctor.

## Week 1: The Long Middle and How Long (Before You Begin, chapters 1 and 2)

- **Open:** What is the longest you have ever waited for something? It can be something small.
- **Read together:** Psalm 13.
- **Discuss:**
  1. The book names four hard parts of waiting: not knowing, time, comparison and silence. Which do you recognize most?
  2. How were you taught to pray when things were hard? How does Psalm 13 compare?
  3. Why do you think laments like Psalm 13 are included in the Bible?
- **Practice:** Try the five-part lament from chapter 2 this week: turn to God, say what is wrong, ask, remember, choose trust.

## Week 2: Abraham, Sarah and Joseph (chapters 3 and 4)

- **Open:** Have you ever been let down by someone who promised to help?
- **Read together:** Genesis 18:10-14 and Genesis 40:20-23.
- **Discuss:**
  1. Sarah laughed when she heard the promise. What do you make of her laugh?
  2. Where is the line between taking sensible steps and taking a harmful shortcut?
  3. Joseph was faithful and stayed in prison. How does that sit with you?
- **Practice:** Name one step that is yours to take this week, and one thing that is only yours to wait for.

## Week 3: Hannah and the Manna (chapters 5 and 6)

- **Open:** Is there a day or season of the year that is harder for you than others?
- **Read together:** 1 Samuel 1:9-18 and Exodus 16:13-21.
- **Discuss:**
  1. Elkanah meant well. Why did his words not help? What helps more?
  2. Hannah's face changed before her circumstances did. Have you experienced something like that?
  3. What is the difference between planning for tomorrow and living in tomorrow?
- **Practice:** Each evening this week, write down one piece of "today's manna".

## Week 4: Lazarus and Babylon (chapters 7 and 8)

- **Open:** What is something you have put on hold until your waiting is over?
- **Read together:** John 11:1-6 and 11:32-36; Jeremiah 29:4-7.
- **Discuss:**
  1. John says Jesus loved the family, so he stayed two days. How do you hear that?
  2. What does it mean to you that Jesus wept?
  3. What would "building houses in Babylon" look like in your life right now?
- **Practice:** Do one small thing this week to "plant a garden" in your current season.

## Week 5: The Saturday and the Answer That Is Not Yes (chapters 9 and 10)

- **Open:** What does Easter Saturday usually feel like to you, if you notice it at all?
- **Read together:** Luke 23:50-56; 2 Corinthians 12:7-10; Hebrews 11:13-16.
- **Discuss:**
  1. In what ways is much of life lived "on Saturday"?
  2. How do you react to the idea that people in Hebrews 11 died without receiving what was promised?
  3. Is it possible to ask boldly for something and also be at peace if the answer is no? What makes that hard?
- **Practice:** Take a real rest this week, even for an afternoon, as an act of trust.

## Week 6: Waiting Together and Those Who Wait (chapters 11 and 12, and the letter)

- **Open:** Who has held up your arms in a hard season?
- **Read together:** Exodus 17:8-13; Isaiah 40:27-31; Luke 2:36-38.
- **Discuss:**
  1. What makes it hard to let others into your waiting? What could make it easier?
  2. Soaring, running, walking: which describes your current season?
  3. What kind of person do you hope this season is making you?
- **Close:** Pray for one another by name, for what each person is waiting for, and for strength to walk without fainting.

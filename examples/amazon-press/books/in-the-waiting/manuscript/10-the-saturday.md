# 9. The Saturday

Christians tell the story of Easter in two great days. Good Friday, the day of the cross. Easter Sunday, the day of the empty tomb.

But there is a day in between.

The Gospels say almost nothing about it. After Jesus died, a man named Joseph of Arimathea asked for his body, wrapped it in linen and laid it in a tomb cut in rock. Some of the women who had followed Jesus from Galilee watched where he was laid. Luke tells us what they did next:

{{verse: Luke 23:56}}

That is all we are told. They rested. It was the Sabbath, and they kept it.

## The day nobody knew

It is hard for us to feel that Saturday the way the first followers of Jesus felt it, because we know what happened on Sunday. We read the story with the ending already in mind.

They did not. As far as they knew, it was over. Their teacher, the one they had left everything to follow, the one they had hoped would set things right, was dead. They had watched him die. They had seen the tomb. One of their own had betrayed him, and most of the rest had run away. Everything they had hoped for was sealed behind a stone.

And it was the Sabbath, so they could not even do anything. No anointing the body. No traveling home. No busyness to distract from the grief. They rested, because God had commanded rest, on what may have been the worst day of their lives.

## Living in Saturday

A great deal of the Christian life is lived on Saturday.

We know about the cross, the reality of suffering and loss. We have heard about Sunday, the promise that death and sorrow do not get the last word. But much of the time, we live in between. The loss has happened. The promise has not yet been fulfilled. And there is very little we can do but wait.

If you are in a Saturday season, I want to say a few things.

**Saturday is not a lack of faith.** The women at the tomb were among the most faithful followers Jesus had. They had stayed when others ran. They watched him die and watched him buried. They planned to care for his body. Their Saturday was not a failure of devotion; it was what devotion looks like when you cannot see the ending.

**Saturday can include rest.** The women rested, not because they felt like it, but because it was the Sabbath. Sometimes, in the middle of grief or waiting, the most faithful thing we can do is stop. Sleep. Eat. Sit. Let others carry what they can. Rest is not giving up.

**Saturday is honest.** No one in the story pretended it was Sunday. When the women went to the tomb at dawn, they took spices for a body. They were not expecting a resurrection. They were doing the loving, ordinary next thing. They were surprised by what they found.

## Between

No one can promise you that your Saturday will end in your own Sunday morning.

What I can say is that the central story of the Christian faith has a Saturday in it, a day of silence and grief and waiting, between the worst and the best. God did not skip that day. Jesus lay in the tomb through it. His friends lived through it, not knowing.

That means your Saturday is not outside the story of God. It is part of the shape of the story. And Christians hold, as their central hope, that Saturday is not the last day.

## For the waiting room

1. Does your season feel more like Friday, Saturday or Sunday right now? Why?
2. What would rest look like for you in this season, even for one day?

# A Letter to the One Still Waiting

Dear friend,

You have reached the end of this book, and I suspect you are still waiting.

I wish I could tell you how your story ends. I can't. I have tried not to pretend otherwise, because you deserve honesty more than you deserve a neat ending.

But here is what I hope you take with you.

You are not the first to wait, and you are not alone. Abraham and Sarah, Joseph, Hannah, the Israelites in the wilderness, the exiles in Babylon, Martha and Mary, the women on Saturday, Paul, Simeon and Anna: they are all in the waiting room with you. Some of them laughed bitterly. Some of them complained loudly. Some of them tried shortcuts. All of them were held.

Your complaints are welcome. *How long* is a prayer.

Today's bread is enough for today. Tomorrow will have its own.

You can build a house in Babylon. A real life is possible in the middle, even this middle.

Love and delay can be in the same sentence. Jesus wept at the grave before he did anything else.

If the answer is no, God's grace is still enough, and you will still be held.

Let someone hold up your arms. And when you can, hold up someone else's.

On the days when all you can do is walk, walk. That counts.

There is one more psalm I want to leave with you. It was written by someone who had enemies and troubles of their own, who did not know how things would turn out, and who decided, in the middle, to keep looking for God's goodness:

{{verse: Psalm 27:13-14}}

Whoever you are, wherever you are reading this, I hope that one day you will look back on this season and see God in it. And until then, I hope you will find him, again and again, in the middle.

Wishing you strength for the waiting.

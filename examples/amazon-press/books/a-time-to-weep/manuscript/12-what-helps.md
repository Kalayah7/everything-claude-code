# What Helps

This page is for the people around someone who is grieving. If you are grieving, you might share it with friends and family who want to help but do not know how.

## Things that usually help

- **Show up.** Sit with them. You do not need to say anything wise.
- **Say the person's name.** Many grieving people long to hear it. Share a memory.
- **Offer something specific.** Not "let me know if you need anything", but "I'm bringing dinner on Tuesday" or "can I take the kids on Saturday?"
- **Remember the dates.** Birthdays, anniversaries and holidays are hard. Send a message on those days.
- **Keep coming back.** Most support fades after a few weeks. Grief does not. A message at three months, six months and a year means a great deal.
- **Listen more than you speak.** Let them tell the story as many times as they need to.
- **Say "I'm so sorry" and "I don't know what to say".** Both are honest and kind.

## Things that usually hurt

- Explanations: *everything happens for a reason*, *God needed another angel*.
- Comparisons: *I know exactly how you feel*, *at least it wasn't...*
- Timelines: *you should be over this by now*, *it's time to move on*.
- Silver linings: *at least they're not suffering*, *at least you have other children*.
- Avoiding them because you do not know what to say.

## Watch for warning signs

If someone who is grieving cannot eat or sleep for a long time, stops functioning, relies on alcohol or drugs, or talks about not wanting to live, gently encourage them to see a doctor or counselor, and stay with them while they do. In the United States, the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline is available by call or text at any time. Elsewhere, contact a local emergency number or crisis line.

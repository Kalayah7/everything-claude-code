# 9. Grieving With Hope

There is a verse that is sometimes used against grieving Christians. It goes like this:

{{verse: 1 Thessalonians 4:13}}

Perhaps someone has quoted it to you, with the suggestion that a Christian should not be too sad, because they have hope. Perhaps you have said it to yourself, and felt guilty for grieving.

It is worth reading what it actually says.

## Paul expects you to grieve

Paul wrote this letter to a young church in the city of Thessalonica. Some of its members had died, and the others were troubled about what that meant for them.

Paul does not say, *I don't want you to grieve.* He says he does not want them to grieve *like the rest, who have no hope.*

The verse assumes that they will grieve. It does not forbid grief. It describes a kind of grief: grief that has hope inside it. The tears are the same. The ache is the same. What is different is what the grief is held within.

Christian grief is still grief. You can weep, rage, ache and miss someone terribly, and still be grieving with hope. The two do not cancel each other out.

## What the hope is

In the verses that follow, Paul explains the hope he means: because Jesus died and rose again, God will bring with him those who have died in Jesus. He speaks of a day when the dead in Christ will rise, and the living and the dead will be together with the Lord. And he ends:

{{verse: 1 Thessalonians 4:18}}

*Comfort one another.* The hope is meant to be shared, spoken between people, a comfort offered in community. It is not meant to be used as a rule to stop someone crying.

## If your hope feels thin

Many grieving Christians find that their hope feels very thin in the first months. They believe it, or want to, but it does not feel like much of a comfort compared with the empty chair at the table.

That is all right. Hope in this verse is not a feeling you have to produce. It rests on what Paul believed God has done in raising Jesus. On the days when you cannot feel it, you can lean on others who can hold it for you. That is part of what *comfort one another* means.

## A word about hard deaths

Some deaths raise painful questions for Christians: deaths by suicide, deaths of people who did not share the faith, deaths of babies and young children. If one of these is your grief, you may have heard people speak with confidence about where your loved one is.

No book, and no person, can see what God sees. Your loved one is in God's hands, and those are merciful hands. A pastor you trust can sit with you in these questions. If your loved one died by suicide, please know that there is no shame in their death or in your grief, and that support groups for suicide loss exist in many places. A grief counselor can help you find one.

## When you are ready

Has anyone used faith to hurry your grief? What would grieving *with* hope, rather than *instead of* grieving, look like for you?

*God, I am grieving. Some days I can hardly feel any hope at all. Hold my hope for me when I cannot, and give me people who will comfort me with it gently. Amen.*

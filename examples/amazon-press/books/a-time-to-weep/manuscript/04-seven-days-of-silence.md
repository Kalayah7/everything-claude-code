# 3. Seven Days of Silence

Job lost almost everything in a single day: his flocks, his servants and all ten of his children. Not long after, his own body was struck with painful sores. He sat among the ashes, scraping his skin with a piece of broken pottery.

Then three of his friends heard what had happened, and they came to him.

## What the friends got right

The book of Job tells us what they did when they arrived:

{{verse: Job 2:12-13}}

They wept aloud. They tore their robes. They sat on the ground with him. And for seven days and seven nights, nobody said a word, because they saw that his grief was very great.

It is one of the most beautiful pictures of comfort in the Bible. Job's friends did not explain anything. They did not offer advice. They did not try to cheer him up. They simply came, and stayed, and were silent with him.

## What the friends got wrong

Then, after the seven days, they started talking.

For most of the rest of the book, Job's friends make speeches. They tell him that God punishes the wicked and blesses the good, so Job must have done something to deserve this. They urge him to repent. They defend God by blaming Job.

At the end of the book, God speaks, and he is not pleased with the friends. He says they have not spoken of him what is right, as Job did. Job, who had cried out, complained and questioned, was closer to the truth than the friends who had tidy explanations.

## The things people say

You have probably heard some things in these months that hurt. Perhaps people said them with love. They still hurt.

*Everything happens for a reason.*
*God needed another angel.*
*At least they are not suffering anymore.*
*At least you had them for so long.*
*I know exactly how you feel.*
*You need to be strong for the children.*

Like Job's friends, the people who say these things usually mean well. They are uncomfortable with your pain and want to fix it. But you do not have to accept their explanations. God himself did not accept the explanations of Job's friends.

You are allowed to be like Job: honest, questioning, even angry, and still turned toward God. Many people find it a relief to know that the Bible sides with the grieving man against his tidy comforters.

## Finding your seven-days friends

What Job's friends did in the first seven days is worth looking for. You may already have such people: the friend who comes over and sits with you without needing to talk, the neighbor who mows the lawn and leaves, the person who says, *I don't know what to say, but I'm here.*

If you have friends like that, lean on them. If people around you want to help but do not know how, you might show them the page called *What Helps* near the end of this book.

## When you are ready

Has anyone said something that hurt you in your grief? Who has comforted you just by being there?

*God, some of the words people say hurt me. Thank you for the ones who simply sit with me. Help me be honest with you, the way Job was, and send me friends who will stay. Amen.*

# 1. A Time to Weep

The book of Ecclesiastes contains one of the best-known poems in the Bible. It begins like this:

{{verse: Ecclesiastes 3:1}}

And a few lines later:

{{verse: Ecclesiastes 3:4}}

People often read this poem at funerals. It is a fitting choice. Look at what it leaves out.

## There is a time to weep

It does not say there is a time to stop weeping. It does not say how long the time to weep should last. It simply says that weeping has its time, just as laughing does, and mourning does, and dancing does. Weeping is not a failure to move on. It is one of the seasons of a human life, and Scripture names it without embarrassment.

In the first months of grief, many people feel pressure to be past it. The funeral is over. The casseroles stop arriving. People return to their normal lives and, gently or not, expect you to return to yours. Someone asks if you are "doing better". Someone at church tells you to rejoice. Your employer gives you three days.

Ecclesiastes gives you something else: permission. This is a time to weep. You are not doing it wrong.

## Grief in your own way

Grief does not look the same in everyone. Some people cry often. Some hardly cry at all, and feel guilty about that. Some feel numb for weeks. Some feel angry, at the doctors, at the person who died, at God, at themselves. Some feel relief, especially after a long illness, and then feel ashamed of the relief. Some have good days that come out of nowhere, and then a song in a shop flattens them.

All of this is normal. There is no correct way to grieve, and the poem does not prescribe one. A time to weep can include all of it.

## God in the seasons

Soon after the poem, the writer adds:

{{verse: Ecclesiastes 3:11}}

The writer of Ecclesiastes admits there is a great deal he cannot understand. People cannot find out the work God has done from beginning to end. He does not pretend to explain loss. This does not mean that the death was beautiful, or that it was meant to be. The writer is admitting how little he can see, not explaining your loss.

But he says God has set eternity in the human heart. Perhaps that is part of why death hurts so much. Something in us knows that love should not end, that the people we love should not simply be gone. Grief is, in part, the ache of eternity in a heart that has just been broken by time.

You do not have to understand why this happened. You do not have to be past it. This is a time to weep, and God is not absent from it.

## When you are ready

What has grief looked like for you so far? Is there anything about it that you have felt you were doing "wrong"?

*God, this is a time to weep. I do not know how long it will last. Let me weep without shame, and stay with me while I do. Amen.*

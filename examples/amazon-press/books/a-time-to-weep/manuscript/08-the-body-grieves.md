# 7. The Body Grieves Too

Grief is not only in the heart. It lives in the body.

Many people are surprised by how physical grief is. Exhaustion that sleep does not fix. No appetite, or eating without noticing. A tight chest. Aches. Getting sick more easily. Forgetting things, losing keys, reading the same paragraph five times. Some people call it grief fog.

The psalms describe this, too:

{{verse: Psalm 6:6-7}}

The writer is not describing a mood. He is describing a body worn down by grief.

## Being gentle with yourself

If your body is struggling, you are not weak. You are grieving. Here are a few gentle things that many grieving people find help a little. They are not a cure for grief, and nothing here replaces a doctor's advice.

- **Eat something small and regular,** even if you are not hungry. Accept the meals people bring.
- **Drink water** through the day.
- **Rest when you can.** If sleep will not come, lying down quietly can still be restful.
- **Go outside for a few minutes** each day if you can, even just to the end of the street.
- **Lower your expectations.** You will not get as much done for a while. That is all right.
- **Let people help with practical things:** shopping, cleaning, paperwork, children, phone calls.

And please see a doctor if you are not eating or sleeping for a long stretch, if you feel unwell, or if you find yourself relying on alcohol or anything else to get through. A doctor can help with the body's side of grief, and seeing one is a kind thing to do for yourself.

## The firsts

One part of grief catches many people off guard: the firsts. The first birthday without them. The first holiday. The first anniversary. The first time you reach for the phone to call them and remember. The first time you hear their song.

These days can be very hard, and they can come with little warning. For the ones you can see coming, it may help to decide in advance who you will be with, and to let yourself change traditions, keep them, or do nothing at all.

The writer of Psalm 6 also says, a little later, that the LORD has heard the sound of his weeping. The tears that flood the bed at night are not lost. They are heard.

## When you are ready

How has grief shown up in your body? What is one small, kind thing you could do for your body today?

*God, my body is tired from grief. You hear my weeping, even at night. Help me be gentle with myself, and let me accept the help I am given. Amen.*

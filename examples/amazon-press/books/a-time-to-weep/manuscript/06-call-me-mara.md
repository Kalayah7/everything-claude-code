# 5. Call Me Mara

The book of Ruth opens with a string of funerals.

A woman named Naomi had moved with her husband and two sons from Bethlehem to the land of Moab, to escape a famine. There, her husband died. Her sons married Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth. About ten years later, both sons died too.

Naomi was left in a foreign land with no husband, no sons and two widowed daughters-in-law.

## Honest bitterness

When she heard that the famine back home had ended, Naomi decided to return to Bethlehem. Ruth insisted on coming with her. When the two women arrived, the whole town was stirred, and the women of Bethlehem asked, *Is this Naomi?*

Her answer is one of the most honest moments in the Bible:

{{verse: Ruth 1:20-21}}

The name Naomi means "pleasant". Mara means "bitter". She was saying: do not call me pleasant anymore. Call me bitter. I went away full, and God has brought me back empty.

Naomi does not hide her grief or tidy it up. She blames God openly, in front of the whole town. She feels emptied out.

The remarkable thing is that the Bible lets her say it. No one in the story rebukes her. The narrator does not correct her theology. Her bitterness is recorded, and the story goes on.

## You can say it too

If you feel bitter, you can say so. If you feel emptied, you can say so. If you are angry with God, you can tell him. Grief can make the things we used to believe feel hollow, and that is frightening. But naming it honestly, as Naomi did, keeps you in the conversation with God rather than pretending.

Some people are afraid that admitting anger at God will push him away. Naomi's story suggests otherwise. She called herself bitter and went on living in God's story.

## The one who stayed

Naomi said she came home empty. But she did not come home alone.

Back on the road from Moab, Naomi had told both her daughters-in-law to go home to their families. Orpah, with tears, went. Ruth would not:

{{verse: Ruth 1:16}}

Naomi, in her grief, could not see Ruth as anything that made her less empty. But Ruth was there, walking beside her, refusing to leave.

The book of Ruth goes on to tell how, in time, Naomi held a grandson in her arms. That ending did not undo her losses. Her husband and sons were still gone. But it is worth knowing that the woman who said *call me bitter* is not abandoned at the end of chapter one.

You do not need to look for a happy ending now. But you might look, gently, for your Ruth: the person who is walking beside you, even if you cannot feel grateful for them yet.

## When you are ready

If you gave yourself a new name for this season, what would it be? Who is walking beside you?

*God, I feel emptied. I am telling you honestly, the way Naomi did. Thank you for the people who will not leave me. Help me see them, even now. Amen.*

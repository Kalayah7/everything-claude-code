# 2. Near to the Brokenhearted

There is a verse in the thirty-fourth psalm that grieving people have held onto for thousands of years:

{{verse: Psalm 34:18}}

It is a short sentence. It makes no promise about when the pain will ease. It does not say that the broken heart will be mended by a certain date. It says one thing: God is near.

## Where God is

Many people in grief feel that God has gone far away. Prayers that once came easily now feel like they hit the ceiling. Church may be painful: the songs too cheerful, the people too eager to say the right thing. You may wonder whether God is there at all.

The psalm's claim is the opposite of what grief feels like. It says that when your heart is broken, God is not further away. He is closer. He is *near to those who have a broken heart*.

You may not feel it. That is all right. The verse does not say that the brokenhearted will feel God near. It says that he is. It is a statement about God, not about your feelings. On the days when you cannot sense anything at all, it is still true.

## A crushed spirit

The second half of the verse speaks of those who have a *crushed spirit*. That is a good description of what deep grief can feel like. Not only sad, but flattened. Pressed down. Unable to get up.

The psalm says God saves people like that. In this context, that means God rescues and holds the crushed, not that he scolds them for not getting up faster.

If you feel crushed, you are exactly the kind of person this verse was written for.

## Honest company

Psalm 34 was written by someone who knew trouble. Its heading links it to a dangerous time in David's life, and the psalm is full of people who are afraid, poor and in distress. It does not pretend that the righteous never suffer. Later in the same psalm it says plainly that the righteous have many afflictions.

So this is not a verse from someone who has never hurt. It is a word from inside trouble, to people inside trouble: God is near you here.

## Letting God be near

You do not have to do anything to make God near. But some people find small ways to make room for that nearness in grief:

- **Saying their loved one's name to God.** Telling him about the person: what you miss, what you wish you had said.
- **Sitting in silence.** Not trying to pray words. Just sitting, knowing you are not alone.
- **Praying someone else's words.** On days when you have no words, the psalms can speak for you. You can read one aloud as your prayer.
- **Letting others pray for you.** It is all right to tell a friend, "I can't pray right now. Will you pray for me?"

## When you are ready

Does God feel near or far right now? Can you tell him honestly?

*God, my heart is broken. I do not feel you near, but your word says you are. Stay close to me in this, even when I cannot feel it. Amen.*

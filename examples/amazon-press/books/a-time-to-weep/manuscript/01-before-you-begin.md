# Before You Begin

Someone you love has died.

Perhaps it was sudden. Perhaps it came at the end of a long illness, after months of hospital rooms and hard decisions. Perhaps it was your mother or father, your husband or wife, your child, your brother or sister, your closest friend. Perhaps it was a pregnancy that ended before you could hold the baby. Perhaps the relationship was complicated, and your grief is tangled with things that were never said.

Whatever happened, you are now living in a world with a hole in it. And the world, for the most part, expects you to keep going.

This is a short book for the first months. It is written for days when you cannot concentrate, when reading more than a few pages feels impossible, when you are not sure what you believe or how you feel. Each chapter is short. You can read one and put the book down for a week. You can read them out of order. You can read one sentence and close it.

## What this book will not do

It will not tell you that everything happens for a reason. It will not tell you how long grief should last, or what stage you should be in. It will not tell you where your loved one is now, or explain why they died. It will not hurry you toward feeling better.

It will sit with you beside some of the grieving people in the Bible, people who wept, shouted, sat in silence, and refused to be comforted, and it will look at how God met them.

## A word about care

Grief is not an illness. It is what love feels like when someone is gone, and it can be very heavy. But sometimes grief brings things that need extra help: being unable to eat or sleep for weeks, being unable to function at all, using alcohol or other things to get through the day, or thinking about not wanting to live. If any of that is true for you, please talk to a doctor, a grief counselor or a pastor soon.

If you are having thoughts of ending your life, please reach out right away. In the United States you can call or text 988 at any time. Elsewhere, call your local emergency number or a crisis line, or tell someone near you today.

## How to use this book

At the end of each chapter, under *When you are ready*, there is one gentle question and a short prayer. You do not have to answer the question. You do not have to pray the prayer. They are there if they help.

Bible quotations come from the World English Bible, a modern public-domain translation, which uses the name Yahweh where many translations print "the LORD".

There is a short guide at the back for grief groups, and a page called *What Helps* that you might want to share with the people around you.

Read this however you can. A sentence counts.

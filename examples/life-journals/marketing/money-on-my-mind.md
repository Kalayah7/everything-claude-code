## Description (conversation)

Can I ask you something? When did you last open your banking app without your stomach dropping?

If the honest answer is "I can't remember", this was made for you.

Maybe the mail sits unopened on the counter. Maybe you check your balance ten times a day, or
you have not checked it in a month. Maybe you earn enough and still lie awake doing sums at
3 a.m. And maybe you have told nobody, because it feels like everyone else has this figured out.

They have not. About two out of three adults in the United States say money is a big source of
stress. And researchers who looked at 65 studies found that money trouble and poor mental
health show up together again and again. Each one makes the other harder. When you feel low,
it is harder to open the bill. When the bill is unopened, you feel worse.

So here is the thing nobody tells you: you are not bad with money. You are stressed, and stress
makes money harder. That is a loop, and a loop can be loosened from either side.

**That is what this journal does.**

It is 30 days. Each day is one page with a short reading, two questions and one small step.
The steps really are small. One day, the step is to look at one number for five minutes. On
another you put the scary envelope where you can see it, and you do not have to open it yet.

- Week 1, Notice: where money stress lives in your body, and where your money beliefs came from.
- Week 2, Look: gently, one number at a time. What comes in, what must go out, what you owe.
- Week 3, Untangle: shame, comparing yourself to others, spending to feel better, hard talks.
- Week 4, Steady: one small system, a weekly 15-minute money check, and a plan for bad days.

**"Is this just another budget planner?"**
No. Budget planners assume you can already look at your numbers. This starts one step earlier,
with the fear that keeps you from looking. There are four simple worksheets inside, and you
reach them in week 2, after you have had a week of practice with the fear.

**"Will this fix my money problems?"**
Honestly, no journal can promise that. It will not pay a bill or make debt vanish. What it can
do is help you look, name what you feel and take one small step a day. Studies of this kind of
writing find a small but real benefit for many people. For most of us, looking is the part we
have been stuck on.

**"What if I miss a day?"**
Then you pick it up the next day. Nothing in here is graded.

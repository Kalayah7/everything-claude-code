## Description (conversation, to the parent)

Has your teenager started saying "I'm fine" in that voice that means the opposite?

You ask about school and get one word. The bedroom door is closed more often. You can tell
something is heavy, and you cannot find a way in.

You are not imagining it, and it is not only your child. In the CDC's 2023 national survey,
4 out of 10 high school students said they had felt sad or hopeless for two weeks or more.
About 7 in 10 teens told Pew Research they feel pressure to get good grades. Nearly half say
social media is mostly bad for people their age.

Most teenagers will not say any of this out loud, least of all to a parent. Many of them will
write it down, though, if nobody is going to read it.

**That is what this journal is for.**

It is called Not Just Me because that is what a struggling teenager most needs to hear. It
runs for 30 days. Each day is one page with a short, honest reading about one real problem,
two questions and one small thing to try. It takes about ten minutes, and it does not preach.

- Week 1, Pressure: grades, the pile of work, perfectionism, test nerves, rest.
- Week 2, People: fitting in, friend drama, loneliness, bullying, dating, saying no.
- Week 3, Phone, body and sleep: comparing online, mean messages, body image, sleep, anger, worry.
- Week 4, Home and what is next: family stress, expectations, identity, the future, and how to ask for help.

**"Will my teenager actually use it?"**
We cannot promise that. Here is what we did to give it a chance. It is written the way a good
older cousin would talk, in short and plain words. It tells them their feelings are normal
before it suggests anything. It never says "just think positive". And it is private. Please do
not read it unless they offer. A journal that might be read does not get written in.

**"Is it a replacement for counseling?"**
No, and it says so on its own pages. Studies of this kind of writing with teenagers find a
small but real benefit on average. That is worth having, but it is not treatment. Day 27
explains the signs that mean a person needs more help than a journal can give. Day 28 gives
them the exact words to use to ask an adult. The help page lists 988 and the Crisis Text Line.

**"What if I am worried right now?"**
Do not wait for a journal. Talk to your child's doctor or school counselor. If you think they
may be in danger, call or text 988.

- **What age?** About 13 to 18. The words and sentences are kept short and plain on purpose.
- **Is it religious?** No.
- **Does it cover self-harm?** It tells the reader to take such thoughts seriously, tell an adult and call or text 988. It does not go into detail.
- **Can a counselor use it with a group?** One copy is for one person. Ask about a group license.
- **Is it for the United States only?** The help page lists United States services. The daily pages work anywhere.
